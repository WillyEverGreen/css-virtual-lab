Section 1

SHA-1 Cryptographic Hash & Avalanche Analysis

The primary objective of this experiment is to inspect the internal mechanics of the SHA-1 (Secure Hash Algorithm 1) cryptographic hash function, trace its 80-round compression pipeline across five 32-bit registers (A, B, C, D, E), analyze the message schedule expansion, and empirically verify the Strict Avalanche Criterion (SAC).

Standard Specification & History

Published by NIST in 1995 as FIPS PUB 180-1 (superseding SHA-0 with a 1-bit circular shift in message expansion), SHA-1 produces a deterministic 160-bit (20-byte) message digest formatted as a 40-character hexadecimal string. While retired by NIST for digital signatures due to collision attacks (SHAttered, 2017), understanding SHA-1 is essential for modern hash design and HMAC constructions.

1. Big-Endian Padding

Converts input to UTF-8, appends a single 1 bit (0x80), pads to 448 mod 512, and appends original 64-bit length in big-endian network byte order.

2. 80-Step Compression

Expands 16 input words into 80 words (W0..W79) and processes 4 distinct 20-step rounds modifying five 32-bit working variables (A, B, C, D, E).

3. Avalanche Diffusion

Demonstrates how flipping a single input bit propagates through the 80 rounds to invert approximately 50% (~80 bits) of the 160-bit digest.

Section 2

Mathematical Architecture & Compression Pipeline

SHA-1 is based on the Merkle-Damgard iterated construction. Messages are processed in 512-bit blocks. An internal 160-bit state vector initialized to standard constants is updated sequentially by each block through 80 step operations.

1. Initial Hash State Registers

The algorithm maintains five 32-bit chaining registers initialized to standard big-endian constants (FIPS 180-1):

H0 = 0x67452301
H1 = 0xEFCDAB89
H2 = 0x98BADCFE
H3 = 0x10325476
H4 = 0xC3D2E1F0

2. Message Schedule Expansion (16 Words → 80 Words)

Each 512-bit message block is split into 16 big-endian 32-bit words (W[0] to W[15]). For rounds 16 to 79, words are expanded using circular left rotation:

For t = 16 to 79:
  W[t] = ROTL( W[t-3] ⊕ W[t-8] ⊕ W[t-14] ⊕ W[t-16] , 1 )

Note: The circular 1-bit left shift (ROTL 1) was the key amendment introduced in SHA-1 over SHA-0, which significantly strengthened resistance against early differential cryptanalysis.

3. 80-Round Compression Functions & Constants

The 80 step operations are partitioned into 4 rounds of 20 steps each. Each round utilizes a specific logical function and additive constant:

Round Steps (t) Non-linear Function f(t, B, C, D) Constant K(t) Operation Type
Round 1 0 – 19 (B & C) | (~B & D) 0x5A827999 (230 × √2) Choice (Ch)
Round 2 20 – 39 B ⊕ C ⊕ D 0x6ED9EBA1 (230 × √3) Parity (XOR)
Round 3 40 – 59 (B & C) | (B & D) | (C & D) 0x8F1BBCDC (230 × √5) Majority (Maj)
Round 4 60 – 79 B ⊕ C ⊕ D 0xCA62C1D6 (230 × √10) Parity (XOR)

4. Step Transformation Operation

In step t, state variables are updated according to:

TEMP = (ROTL(A, 5) + f(t, B, C, D) + E + W[t] + K[t]) mod 232
E = D
D = C
C = ROTL(B, 30)
B = A
A = TEMP

5. Cryptanalytic Status & SHAttered Attack (2017)

In 2017, researchers from CWI Amsterdam and Google achieved the landmark SHAttered attack, constructing two distinct PDF files that produced the exact same SHA-1 digest using approximately 9 quintillion (9 × 1018) SHA-1 computations (equivalent to 6,500 CPU-years + 110 GPU-years). Consequently, SHA-1 is deprecated for security-sensitive applications in favor of SHA-256 and SHA-3.
